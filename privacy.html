Akashic Chords privacy policy

Last updated: October 19, 2026

Akashic Chords is a songbook and performance app made by Aedryan Gantt (AEDRYANGANTT.com). This policy explains what the app keeps, where it keeps it, and what reaches anyone else.

What stays on your device

  • Your songs, setlists, shows, settings, play history, practice log, notes, pictures, recordings and audio files are stored in the app's storage on your own device.
  • They aren't sent to us. We don't run a server that collects them, and the app has no ads and no tracking.
  • Backups go only where you send them: a file you save, a copy kept in the app's own storage, or a private GitHub repository you set up yourself with your own token. That token is kept only on your device and is sent only to GitHub (api.github.com).

Microphone and camera

  • The app uses the microphone only when you start the tuner or a recording (a take, a voice memo, a quick idea or a show recording), and the camera only when you record video or take a photo of a song.
  • Recordings stay on your device unless you share or back them up yourself.
  • If you link two of your own devices to record together or sync, they connect to each other directly; the short messages that set up that link pass through your own Firebase project.

Online lookups (only when you use them)

  • Rhymes and similar words. With an internet connection, the word you typed is sent to the Datamuse API (api.datamuse.com). Without one, they come from a word list built into the app, and nothing is sent.
  • Finding lyrics, chords or song info. The app opens a web search (Google, YouTube or Spotify) in your browser, with the song title and artist.
  • Venue search. The venue name you type is sent to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service.
  • Google Calendar. Only if you connect a calendar: the app reads that calendar's events with the key you give it.
  • Fonts. Only if you add a font from Google Fonts: the app downloads it from fonts.googleapis.com.

Song requests from the audience (only if you turn them on)

  • In This device only mode, requests stay on your device.
  • In Online mode, requests are kept in your own Firebase project. Each request holds the song the person picked, any name, note or message they typed, and a note of any tip they say they sent.
  • People who make a request sign in anonymously. Firebase gives them a random ID, and no account or email is needed.
  • Tips are paid through Venmo, Cash App, PayPal or Square. Those services have their own privacy policies. The app never sees card or bank details.

Email list (only if you turn it on)

  • If the performer turns on the email list, people on the request page can choose to give their email address, their name, and a recorded agreement to get emails from the performer about shows and music.
  • In Online mode, the signup passes briefly through the performer's own Firebase project. The app then moves it into the performer's library on their device and deletes it from Firebase.
  • The list is used only to email people about what they signed up for. It isn't sold or shared.
  • Anyone can ask to be removed at any time by replying to any email from the performer. They will be removed from the list.
  • If the performer uses their own signup form (a link) instead, that service's privacy policy applies.

Children

The app isn't directed at children under 13. The request page doesn't ask for anyone's age, and the email list is meant for adults.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date.
